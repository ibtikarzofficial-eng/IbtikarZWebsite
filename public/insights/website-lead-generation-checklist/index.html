The 10-Part Website Lead Generation Checklist

Review the ten parts of a service-business website that influence clarity, trust, enquiries, measurement and follow-up.

Use these ten checks to find the weakest link between a website visit and a serious business enquiry.

Website improvement becomes expensive when every suggestion is treated as equally urgent. A new animation, a rewritten headline and a broken enquiry notification are not the same type of problem. This checklist helps you review the complete system in a sensible order.

1. Message clarity

Can a first-time visitor explain what the business does, who it helps and why it matters after a few seconds? If not, fix the headline and supporting copy before adding more features.

2. Audience and problem fit

The page should use problems and language that real customers recognize. Generic copy produces generic attention. Strong copy helps the right visitor self-identify.

3. Offer and next step

Not every visitor is ready to buy. A checkup, estimate or fit review can provide a lower-risk first step, but its scope and response expectation must be clear.

4. Trust evidence

Show who is responsible, how the work is done and what evidence exists. Real screenshots, implementation notes, founder identity and honest limitations are more credible than unsupported superlatives.

5. Mobile usability

Check the real experience on a phone: headings, tap targets, menus, form controls, sticky elements and horizontal scrolling. Mobile traffic is not useful when the action path is uncomfortable.

6. Primary call to action

Every key page should have one obvious action aligned with its purpose. Repeating a clear CTA is useful. Repeating several competing CTAs creates hesitation.

7. Form reliability

Use visible labels, understandable errors, spam protection, storage and notifications. Test the complete submission from more than one device and confirm that the lead can be recovered even if an email notification fails.

8. Source and event tracking

Measure page views, CTA clicks, form starts and confirmed submissions. Capture campaign tags, referrer and landing page. Do not report button clicks as completed leads.

9. Follow-up system

A lead needs an owner, status, next action and response standard. Track qualification, proposals, wins and losses so website traffic can be evaluated by business quality.

10. Speed, search and technical trust

Review HTTPS, mobile performance, page titles, headings, internal links, sitemap, robots, Search Console and broken pages. Technical quality supports trust, discoverability and conversion.

Best next step: score every area from 0 to 10, choose the three lowest scores and improve those before expanding the website.
